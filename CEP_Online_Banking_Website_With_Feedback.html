01 · About the project

Making digital banking safer for everyone

Digital banking makes payments quick and convenient. At the same time, users need to recognise suspicious messages, calls and payment requests before acting on them.

🎯

Aim

To understand online banking awareness and encourage safer digital payment practices in the community.

🔍

Objectives

Assess service usage, OTP/PIN/CVV knowledge, phishing awareness, scam recognition and everyday security habits.

🌐

Scope

The project studies general awareness and self-reported practices. It does not technically test banking systems.

Why this project matters

Fraudsters may use SMS, messaging apps, phone calls, social media, fake websites and QR-code prompts. Simple safety habits can reduce avoidable risks.

How the study was conducted

A descriptive survey was conducted using a Google Forms questionnaire. The report analyses the response summary of 30 participants through counts, percentages and descriptive observations.

04 · Know the threats

Common online banking scams

Scammers may pretend to be trusted people or organisations to pressure users into sharing information or approving payments.

✉️

Phishing messages

Fake SMS, emails or messages that lead to fraudulent websites or ask for confidential details.

📞

Vishing / fake calls

A caller impersonates bank staff or customer care and pressures someone to reveal an OTP or other details.

🔗

Fake banking links

Unexpected links may imitate a bank website. Check the address and use the bank's official app or website instead.

💸

Fake UPI requests

A payment or collect request may be presented as a way to receive money. Read the request carefully before approving.

▦

QR-code tricks

Scanning a QR code or approving a request can initiate a payment. A QR code is not automatically a way to receive money.

🎧

Fake customer care

Fraudulent support listings may connect users to scammers. Find contact details through official bank channels.

05 · Protect yourself

Your secure banking checklist

Simple habits can help protect your account and personal information.

✓Never share your OTP, PIN, CVV or password with anyone.
✓Do not click unexpected banking links or test unknown links.
✓Do not install remote-access apps at a caller’s request.
✓Check the recipient name and amount before approving a payment.
✓Verify callers through the bank’s official app, website or phone number.
✓Avoid banking over public or untrusted Wi-Fi.
✓Keep your phone and banking apps updated; use a screen lock.
✓Check transaction alerts and account statements regularly.
✓Report suspected fraud quickly to your bank and appropriate official channels.
Remember: If a caller asks for your OTP, refuse, end the interaction and independently contact your bank using an official channel.
06 · Proposed community activity

Turn awareness into action

The report proposes a short awareness session built around practical examples and safe decision-making.

STEP 01

Introduce scam types

Explain phishing, impersonation calls and payment-request risks.

STEP 02

Inspect suspicious messages

Demonstrate warning signs without opening unknown links.

STEP 03

Practise safe responses

Use role-play for handling a caller asking for an OTP.

STEP 04

Share a checklist

End with simple habits and clear reporting guidance.

The activity should not ask participants to share personal banking information or display real account details.

07 · Conclusion & recommendations

Safer banking starts with informed decisions

The survey suggests that respondents regularly use digital banking, particularly UPI, and many have heard about phishing. However, gaps remain in credential awareness, scam-identification confidence, transaction monitoring and safe use of public Wi-Fi.

Key recommendations

Conduct short awareness sessions with fictional examples; repeat the rule never to disclose confidential credentials; teach users to end suspicious calls and verify independently; explain UPI collect requests and QR-code risks; encourage regular transaction checks, app locks, software updates and trusted network use; and provide clear reporting information.

Limitations & future scope

The study is based on 30 responses and is not demonstrated to be a representative sample. Future work could include a wider community, a pre- and post-awareness quiz, multilingual material and feedback from banking or cyber-safety educators.

Secure online banking requires both responsible technology use and informed decisions. Pause before acting on urgent messages, protect confidential credentials and verify requests through official channels.
